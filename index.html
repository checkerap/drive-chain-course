Your first week as the shop's computer person

You've just started at Drive Chain, a small bike-repair shop. The mechanics are brilliant with a spoke key and hopeless with a keyboard, so the computer side is now your job.

This isn't a video course or a quiz. It's real work on a real server: you log in over SSH, find files, fix the database, change code, push to GitHub, open pull requests, and keep things running. The kind of work a junior developer does in their first weeks.

No experience needed. You need a Windows PC or a Mac, a GitHub account, and some curiosity.

How it works

Your colleague Jo hands you work one ticket at a time, as GitHub issues.

1 · A ticket arrives

Jo opens a GitHub issue: what's wrong, what Sam (the owner) needs, and enough explanation to get you started.

2 · You do the work

On the workshop computer, a real Linux server in a data centre, typing commands from your own computer.

3 · Comment /check

Jo looks at the server and the repo. Either you get the next ticket, or Jo tells you what's still missing.

  • Stuck? Comment /hint for a nudge.
  • Every ticket adds to your cheat sheet.
  • The shop's data is generated just for you.
  • Things will break. Fixing them is part of the job.
